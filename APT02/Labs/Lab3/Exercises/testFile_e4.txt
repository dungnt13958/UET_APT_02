Multi-robot systems (MRSs) offer superior flexibility, scalability, and fault tolerance compared to single-agent robotic architectures. By leveraging direct or indirect (stigmergic) communication, simple robotic units can coordinate their actions to solve large-scale or distributed problems. This paper explores the core dimensions of MRS design, focusing on centralized versus decentralized control architectures, multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) algorithms, and real-time environmental awareness. We examine current breakthroughs—including game-theoretic approaches, graph neural networks, and large language model (LLM)-driven functional expression coordination—while highlighting key open challenges such as communication latency, safety guarantees, and hardware heterogeneity. Finally, we discuss emerging real-world applications in disaster response, autonomous logistics, simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), and cooperative transport.
